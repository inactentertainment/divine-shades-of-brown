Who to approach—and what to bring

Artists, musicians & songwriters

For collaboration, start with an artist’s official business channel or a producer whose published work fits your project. For bookings, use the artist’s listed booking representative.

Bring a short introduction, two or three relevant samples, your budget or collaboration proposal, and a clear timeline.

Actors & performers

For auditions, follow verified casting notices and the named casting team’s official submission process. For representation, consult the agency’s published submission guidelines.

Bring a current headshot, résumé and concise reel. Check requirements before sending materials.

Filmmakers & crew

Look for producers, directors, editors and crew with relevant credits. Official fellow profiles, screenings and industry events can provide useful starting points.

Bring a one-page project outline, your role, production dates, location, compensation and links to prior work.

Emerging voices

Start with training programs, public showcases and local creative communities. Build connections through thoughtful feedback and a specific, realistic project.

Verify the organization and opportunity directly. Public listings do not guarantee availability, selection or a response.
