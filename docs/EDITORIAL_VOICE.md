# Divine Shades of Brown editorial voice

Our motto: **Build the change. Practice excellence. Care for each other.**

Write for visitors. Explain creative names in plain language. Aim for short sentences and roughly sixth-grade readability without talking down to readers. Use concrete examples, clear headings, reflection questions, and practical next steps.

Center Black America and the wider African diaspora. Uphold care, honest prices, reliability, high quality, fair feedback, and responsible ownership. Personal responsibility and structural barriers both matter. Never describe Black people as inherently deficient or turn shared standards into blame.

Use an original voice: hopeful, direct, warm, dignified, and occasionally poetic. Do not imitate a living speaker's distinctive style or combine leaders into a fake quotation. Cite Barack Obama, Malcolm X, Martin Luther King Jr., and other Black voices accurately and in context. Include Black women and varied diaspora perspectives.

Quoted speakers and authors come from the Black diaspora. Use brief, verified excerpts with visible author names, author/date citations, actual online locators, and reference links. Prefer primary archives, authors, publishers, and Black-led subject resources. No guessed page numbers, fabricated quotes, or extended copyrighted book passages. Clearly distinguish DSB recommendations from source claims. Historical voices are not evidence of today's statistics. State publication dates; do not call a handpicked topic list a measured ranking.

Human emoji tones are visitor-selected, stored locally, and never inferred from identity. Preserve neutral symbols where skin-tone variants do not exist. Personal tools remain device-local until a secure backend is verified.
