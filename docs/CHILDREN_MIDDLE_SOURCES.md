# DSB Middle Years: source and editorial notes

Prepared 12 October 2026. Exactly 100 unique selections, ranked editorially. These ranks are DSB's reading sequence, not an existing award body's ranking and not a measured literacy assessment.

Age ranges and reading-demand labels are editorial browsing guidance. A read-aloud can support understanding of a book that a child cannot yet decode independently. No Lexile, grade-equivalent, clinical, or guaranteed learning claim is implied. The practical introductions, questions, activities, and DSB takes are original editorial writing.

The selection begins with visual storytelling, family, sport, history, and fantasy, then broadens into scientific and mathematical inquiry, poetry, diaspora settings, and continuing series. Sequel choices intentionally sit later than first volumes. Black authors are central throughout; a few books have credited non-Black collaborators or illustrators (for example, William Kamkwamba with Bryan Mealer). The full author credit is retained rather than disguising collaboration.

Metadata verification used publisher pages, author catalogues, primary award records, and library records. Dates identify first publication of the named work or named adaptation, not the current edition's print date. ISBNs are optional: only identifiers observed for the correct work were included. Cover edition differences are possible.

US/UK title note: Anne-Marie Imafidon's *How to Be a Math Wizard* is the US title of *How to Be a Maths Whizz* (2020). Kereen Getten's US spelling is *When Life Gives You Mangos*; the UK title uses *Mangoes*. The Boy Who Harnessed the Wind and Hidden Figures selections are the young-readers adaptations, not the original adult books or picture-book editions.
