# DSB Early Years 100 — editorial/source notes

Compiled 2026-10-12. Exactly 100 distinct works by Black writers or Black retellers; illustrators alone do not determine inclusion. No overlap with the current adult canon. Titles cover birth–7 with adult read-aloud use, beginning readers, and two short-chapter crossovers. Suggested ages and reading-demand tags are editorial browsing guidance, not standardized scores, diagnoses, or claims that every child of an age can decode a title.

The shelf sequence begins with attachment, language and positive identity, expands into family/community and imaginative/historical reading, and ends with selected beginning-reader opportunities. The ordering is an editorial reading invitation; it is not a popularity vote or an externally certified top-100 ranking.

## Verification and limits

Every title was searched individually against title/author bibliographic evidence. Publisher/author/award pages are linked where indexed; primary publisher previews in Google Books and public-library/catalog records supply fallback bibliographic evidence. Donald Crews titles also cross-check the ALA lifetime-award bibliography; Angela Johnson titles cross-check the University of Southern Mississippi de Grummond archival bibliography. Several current publisher pages display reissue dates, so metadata uses the original work year rather than the current ISBN edition date. ISBNs are intentionally sparse and appear only when visible in cited publisher product URLs and checksum-valid. Some ISBNs describe later editions; do not treat them as the first-edition ISBN. No Amazon IDs, cover URLs, sales rankings, or reader ratings were invented. Availability, publisher-cover permissions, and final purchase links still require the owner’s integration workflow.

Adult guidance, activities, and DSB takes are original editorial writing based on premises. They are introductory guides, not 2,000-word reviews, diagnoses, or licensed reproductions of books. History/grief titles explicitly require adult preview and sensitive conversation. Trade beginning readers are distinguished from systematically sequenced decodable phonics materials.
